Yes — **there is a substantial amount of research we can use**, but it supports different parts of your model with very different levels of strength.

For example:

- **Internet pornography and sexual behaviour:** there are systematic reviews covering research from 2000 onward. The evidence supports associations with some sexual behaviours, but researchers explicitly caution against assuming causation.  PubMed+1
- **Online dating:** there is now evidence that dating apps have materially changed young adults' dating/sexual markets, including evidence of increased sexual activity and increased inequality in dating outcomes, particularly among men.  American Economic Association
- **Father involvement:** there are systematic reviews of longitudinal studies finding associations between involved fathering and several positive developmental outcomes.  PubMed
- **Father absence:** more rigorous causal research still finds negative effects on some child outcomes, although the effects are smaller than older observational research suggested.  Annual Reviews
- **Childhood bullying/rejection and LGBT youth:** there is substantial research on stigma, discrimination, relationships and protective factors, although this does **not** establish that childhood experiences cause someone's sexual orientation or gender identity.  PMC
- **Christian sexual theology:** there are contemporary Christian theological statements explicitly grounding male/female sexuality and marriage in Genesis and the broader biblical creation narrative.  Assemblies of God

So I think we can make a **proper reference page** behind the Mermaid diagram rather than having the diagram carry all the claims.

**Hypothesis** → the connections you're proposing between those observations.
